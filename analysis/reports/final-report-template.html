What is actually predictable

Not direction — size

Bitcoin's next move is a coin flip. Its size is not: absolute returns autocorrelate at +0.42 at one minute and are still at +0.25 an hour later. We built a model that exploits this and it beats every standard benchmark — HAR, GARCH, GJR-GARCH and EGARCH, the last three by such a margin that they fall below simply carrying the last hour forward.

Then we asked the question that matters: is it better than what the market already charges? Deribit publishes DVOL, its own 30-day implied volatility. DVOL beat us — RMSE 0.227 against our 0.311. In an encompassing regression our forecast added two points of R², with a negative coefficient, meaning it corrects our own over-reaction rather than adding information.

Being excellent against a 2009 academic benchmark and losing to the market is the whole lesson in one sentence.

Watching an edge die

The clearest thing in the data

We also tested the positioning data Binance publishes free every five minutes: open interest, the long/short ratio of top traders by account and by position size, and the taker buy/sell split. The “smart money” hypothesis says follow them; the crowding hypothesis says fade them. Zero of sixteen features survived multiple-testing correction, and top-trader positioning came in at ρ ≈ 0.005 — indistinguishable from nothing.

Long spot, short the perpetual, collect funding. Delta roughly zero, no view on direction, and in 2021 it paid 30.6% annualised. It is the simplest trade in crypto.

The last twelve months paid 3.4%. A government bond pays comparably without exchange counterparty risk, margin capital, or liquidation risk. The trade was real and it has been competed away — and because funding is published every eight hours, you can watch it happen year by year. That visibility is rare; most edges die quietly.

A number to disbelieve

Computed naively, the carry shows a Sharpe of 17. That figure assumes a perfect, free delta hedge. Add 5% hedge tracking error and it falls to 2.35; at 10%, to 1.18.

Any Sharpe above about 3 in a public strategy should be treated as a modelling artifact until proven otherwise. This one was ours.

What survived

And the test that convinced us

Every lookback beats buy-and-hold on both assets, at every cost level up to 20 bps, with drawdowns roughly halved. The value is concentrated in downside protection: in the 2021–22 bear market every variant lost less than holding, and one was flat against buy-and-hold's −0.85.

That the ordering across lookbacks is nearly identical on two independent assets is what moved this from interesting to credible. A pattern that exists only on Bitcoin is a Bitcoin fact.

The finding we did not expect

A better forecast is a worse position sizer

Our volatility model beats HAR by 30% on forecast accuracy. We then used it to size positions, expecting the good forecast to produce a better strategy. It did the opposite.

Sizing methodSharpeTurnover / year
Simple 20-day realized volatility0.617×
Our LightGBM forecast0.5048×

For an always-long position it is worse still: 0.07 against 0.45. The reason is that the two tasks want opposite things. Forecasting rewards responsiveness; sizing rewards stability. A model tuned to react quickly to changing volatility produces a position that churns, and the churn costs more than the accuracy earns.

We spent days optimising forecast accuracy. It was the wrong objective for the use we had in mind, and no amount of additional accuracy would have fixed it.

How we kept ourselves honest

Two bugs in our own work survived long enough to produce believable wrong answers, and both were caught by a result being implausible rather than by a test failing.

  • A 14-day exchange outage in December 2017 — during the peak of the mania, which cannot be right. It was 20,400 real bars whose timestamps sat seconds inside the minute. Dropping them would have discarded half the most informative month in the series.
  • The first momentum result was contaminated. The signal was built from a cumulative sum of forward returns, so the running total already contained the return being predicted — correlation exactly 1.000. It survived rebuilding, but only because a twelve-month sum is barely moved by one day.

Beyond that: chronological splits only, never random; walk-forward evaluation with every block scored before it is trained on; costs charged everywhere; Benjamini-Hochberg correction when testing 38 indicators at once; and the full parameter grid reported rather than its best cell. Every source file was checksum-verified against the SHA-256 published beside it.

The honest ceiling

Sharpe standard error on this sample is 0.35. The trend result's margin over buy-and-hold is roughly one and a half standard errors — suggestive, not conclusive. Both assets rose enormously across the window, which flatters a long-biased rule. And trend following is a crowded, well-documented premium, not a discovery.

A backtest without slippage, funding or execution risk is not a live result, and nothing here should be mistaken for one.

If you take one thing

The market is efficient in exactly the dimensions you can observe cheaply, and the observable edges decay on a timescale you can measure. What is left over is not a prediction but a risk premium — you get paid for holding something uncomfortable through drawdowns most people will not tolerate, and no model makes that discomfort go away.
